# Workflow Friction Index 2026 — Execution Playbook

Campaign window: 22 November–21 December 2026  
Early access: submissions may arrive before the formal campaign window and must be tagged as early responses.  
Owner: Varia Studio  
Campaign hook: The $10,000 Question  
Campaign identity: Workflow Friction Index 2026  
Audience: soil scientists and laboratories, agronomists, GIS/geospatial analysts, remote-sensing/EO specialists, precision-ag teams, environmental-data and carbon-MRV practitioners.

## 1. Strategic objective

Find one repeated, costly, technically solvable workflow problem with identifiable buyers and a credible path to a paid pilot. This is not a prize draw, grant, giveaway, generic tech survey, or promise to build the most-voted feature.

Public clarification: The $10,000 figure is a thought experiment. Varia is not offering a $10,000 prize, grant or funding.

## 2. The campaign loop

1. Discover: a specialist sees a provocative workflow-specific post.
2. Participate: they complete the three-step form and receive a Workflow Archetype, an estimated annual labour burden and a shareable Friction Card.
3. Play: they earn non-redeemable Community XP for participation actions and unlock four badges.
4. Compare: they see curated, anonymized Friction Wall cards and can select “I have this too”.
5. Refer: they share a personal referral URL and invite relevant peers.
6. Validate: Varia interviews people with recurring, expensive problems.
7. Commit: one shortlisted workflow is tested with a scoped paid-pilot offer.

## 3. Gamification rules

| Action | XP | Badge |
|---|---:|---|
| Submit a valid workflow | 30 | Workflow Scout |
| Vote on a Friction Wall card | 5 | Community Spotter |
| Share a Friction Card | 15 | Signal Booster |
| Share a referral link | 10 | Field Connector |

XP is a participation mechanic, not a scientific score, currency, discount, prize entry or ranking of problem importance. No public leaderboard. Do not reward repeated votes or low-quality submissions. Public cards are manually reviewed and published only when the participant separately opts in. One browser token per vote and server-side IP rate limits are basic abuse controls, not Sybil-proof identity verification.

These are operating targets, not forecasts. Report actual sample composition and source mix. Do not generalize a convenience sample to the entire industry.

## 5. Opportunity score

Score each problem from 1–5, then weight:
- Severity / operational impact: 20%
- Frequency: 15%
- Time and financial burden: 15%
- Demonstrated willingness to pay: 25%
- Reachable buyers / market access: 10%
- Varia technical fit: 15%

Build gate: at least three independent organizations report the same core problem; at least two decision-makers agree to review a concrete proposal; and at least one organization makes a real paid-pilot commitment. Votes alone never satisfy the gate.

## 7. Channel plan

### LinkedIn (primary)
- Founder profile is the primary distribution surface; Varia Studio page is secondary.
- Publish 3–4 substantive posts per week, not daily low-value promotions.
- Use native text posts, simple diagrams/cards and comment-led prompts.
- Each post asks one specific workflow question and gives context before linking to the campaign.
- Reply with a useful follow-up question, not a generic link drop.

### Specialist communities
- Participate before sharing the campaign.
- Follow each group's rules; no unsolicited mass posting.
- Prioritize soil science, lab management, GIS, EO, precision agriculture and environmental-data communities.
- Invite moderators to review the research purpose where required.

### Direct outreach
- Build a hand-curated list of practitioners and budget owners, not scraped bulk email lists.
- Personalize by role and observable workflow.
- One invitation and one respectful follow-up maximum.
- Do not imply the recipient's company has a problem without evidence.
- No cold emails are sent automatically by this package.

### Referral loop
- Each participant receives a unique referral URL.
- Ask for one or two relevant peers, not indiscriminate sharing.
- Referral is counted when a referred person submits; it does not change research scoring.

## 8. Moderation and publication

1. New submissions enter pending status.
2. Reject spam, third-party personal data, confidential material, identifying customer details and unverifiable allegations.
3. Public card requires explicit allow-public-card consent.
4. Use a short paraphrase or quote that removes organization, people, client, location and project identifiers.
5. Keep contact email and follow-up permission out of public card records.
6. A quote in the final report requires separate allow-quote consent.
7. Never publish raw form text automatically.

### Review queue query

    select id, created_at, role, organization_type, country, category,
           workflow_title, problem_description, frequency, hours_per_week,
           people_affected, current_tools, operational_impact,
           paid_for_solution, budget_range, buyer_role, paid_pilot_interest,
           allow_public_card, allow_quote, contact_email, allow_followup
    from public.wfi_submissions
    where moderation_status = 'pending'
    order by created_at asc;

### Publish an anonymized card

Only after manual review and only when allow_public_card is true. Use reviewed, non-identifying wording. Example:

    insert into public.wfi_public_cards
      (submission_id, category, role_label, headline, quote,
       frequency_label, time_lost_label, published)
    select id, category, 'GIS practitioner',
           'Repeated geospatial processing',
           'Reviewed anonymized paraphrase of the workflow problem.',
           'Weekly', 'Several hours / week', true
    from public.wfi_submissions
    where id = '<submission-uuid>'
      and allow_public_card = true
      and moderation_status = 'pending';

Then set the submission status to approved. If unsuitable for publication, keep it private and update the status accordingly.

## 9. Privacy and data handling

- Participation is private by default.
- Follow-up, public wall and report quote permissions are separate opt-ins.
- No file upload is offered; tell participants not to submit confidential or personal information.
- Contact email is stored only when follow-up is explicitly enabled.
- Raw IP addresses are not stored; the edge function uses a one-way hash for basic rate limiting and vote deduplication.
- Respond to access/deletion requests at mah.kzmi21@gmail.com.
- The public report must state sample size, dates, respondent roles, country mix, recruitment channels, missing data and limitations.
- Do not claim statistical representativeness.

## 10. Analytics and daily review

Track source / UTM / referral code; landing visits and CTA clicks; form start, step progression, completion and errors; role, country, organization type and category; recurring problem clusters; frequency, time burden, workarounds and operational impact; budget owner, previous spend and paid-pilot interest; interviews; proposals; buyer responses; and paid commitments.

Daily 15-minute review:
1. Inspect new submissions and spam.
2. Moderate opt-in cards.
3. Update problem clusters and opportunity scores.
4. Reply to comments and relevant referrals.
5. Record buyer conversations and next actions.
6. Publish only evidence-backed observations.

## 11. Stop / pivot rules

- Low traffic: improve distribution before changing the question.
- Many vague submissions: tighten examples and form prompts.
- Many votes but no buyer budget: do not build a product yet.
- Fragmented problems: narrow the audience or workflow.
- No paid commitment: report honestly and continue discovery rather than announcing a product.
