## Post 9 — Cost of friction (Dec 10)

A workflow can feel like “just a few extra hours” until you multiply it by the people doing it and the number of projects.

Six hours a week.
Three people.
Forty-six working weeks.

That's 828 hours a year tied up in one recurring process — before counting delays, rework or downstream errors.

We built a simple calculator to help make the labour burden visible. It's an estimate, not a savings promise.

## Post 10 — Shortlist (Dec 13)

We're moving from collecting workflow friction to identifying the problems that deserve a closer look.

The shortlist will be based on more than votes:
- repeated reports from independent organizations;
- frequency and operational impact;
- existing workarounds and previous spend;

## Comment replies

**When someone describes a problem:**  
That sounds like a real handoff issue. Where does it usually break — at the data format, the metadata, the tool integration or the final output?

**When someone says “same here”:**  
Which part costs you the most time: the repeated task itself, fixing errors, or getting the output into the next team's workflow?

**When someone asks if there's a prize:**  
No prize or grant — the $10,000 is a thought experiment to help surface the problem people would most want to eliminate.

**When someone asks what Varia will build:**  
We haven't decided yet. The point is to identify a repeated problem with operational impact and a real buyer before committing to a product.
